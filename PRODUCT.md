# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Four audiences, one page. In descending order of how ready they are to buy:

- **Upwork buyers** arriving from a proposal or portfolio item. Semi-qualified, already interested, here to check the person is real and the work is real. They convert fastest and they read the least.
- **Cold founders and SMB owners** from LinkedIn or referral. They feel the pain (manual work, dropped leads, a team drowning in tickets) but have no vocabulary for what to buy. They need the page to name their problem before it names its solution.
- **Technical evaluators** — a CTO or lead dev sent to vet capability. They skip the pitch and go straight to the case studies. They are looking for evidence of engineering judgment, not tool lists.
- **Recruiters and agencies** sizing up contract capacity.

The page has to work for a 20-second skim and a 5-minute read at the same time. Nobody is here to browse.

## Product Purpose

A one-page case for hiring Salah Eddine Zaka to build AI systems that produce a
commercial outcome. Success is exactly one action: booking a call. Case studies,
FAQ and stack exist to remove the objections standing between the visitor and
that button, in the order those objections actually arise (is this real? can he
build? what does it cost? is my data safe?).

## Brand Personality

Bold, direct, commercial. Leads with money and outcomes, not technology. Speaks
in claims a client could check, never in adjectives. Short sentences. Verbs over
nouns. First person, no corporate "we".

The bold register lives in the **claims and the typography**, never in chrome.
Loud copy on a calm surface. If the page needs a glow to feel confident, the copy
is not confident enough.

## Anti-references

- **AI-agency template** — neon gradients, glowing orbs, "unlock the power of AI", fake dashboard screenshots, floating 3D shapes.
- **No-code hustler** — n8n canvas screenshots, "$10k/mo automation" energy, Gumroad-style paid-product rows, income screenshots.
- **Corporate consultancy** — handshake stock photography, vague enterprise-speak, navy and gold, "leveraging synergies".
- **Dev-portfolio cliché** — terminal windows, monospace everything, matrix green, "hi, I'm a full-stack dev".

Also banned: any number a visitor cannot verify. One checkable claim beats four
impressive ones.

## Design Principles

1. **Money first, machinery second.** Every section states the commercial outcome before the technology. The stack is proof, not the pitch.
2. **Only verifiable claims ship.** No placeholder text, no invented metrics, no sections advertising things that do not exist. An empty section beats a fake one.
3. **Skimmable at 20 seconds, deep at 5 minutes.** Headlines and first lines carry the whole argument on their own. Detail lives one layer down, in the case studies and the FAQ.
4. **Answer the objection before it is raised.** Cost, safety and process are on the page, not behind a form.
5. **Engineering shows through restraint.** Precision, working links, correct spacing and honest copy are the evidence of craft. Effects are not.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at 4.5:1 or better against its surface, all interactive
elements keyboard reachable with a visible focus ring, headings in true document
order. `prefers-reduced-motion` disables the hero network animation and all
transitions. Colour is never the only carrier of meaning (the orange accent
always pairs with position, weight or a label).
